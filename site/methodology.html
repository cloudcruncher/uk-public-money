How we work out the numbers

In one sentence

Cash, real terms and % of GDP

Cash figures are as published, scaled to £ billion where needed (the scaling is recorded in each number’s source).

Real terms figures are in … prices: cash × deflator in the base year ÷ deflator in that year, using the OBR GDP deflator at market prices (…). Tax-year series are deflated with the financial-year deflator, and say so. Counts of people are never deflated.

% of GDP is the value ÷ nominal GDP for the same financial year from the same OBR vintage × 100. It is not published for series that are not UK-wide (such as PESA Table 10.1, England only; the site's main spending split now uses UK-wide PESA Table 5.2) or for tax-year series.

When a view is not available for a series, the page says so and shows cash instead.

Outturn and forecast

Outturn is what was actually spent or received. Forecast covers forecasts, plans and projections from the publisher (for example OBR forecasts, DWP spring forecasts and HMRC income tax projections). Forecasts are hatched in charts and labelled “Forecast” next to the number. A number derived from any forecast input is itself a forecast. The latest outturn year in this build is ….

Editions and gaps

Publishers revise past years. For each year we use the newest edition that covers it, and every number shows its edition. Older values are kept in the published all-vintages file. Years that no fetched edition covers are gaps with a reason. We never interpolate, carry values forward or back-cast. Coverage starts in ….

Known limits of this slice

  • PESA Table 10.1 is England-only spending on services and is kept as a fallback. The main spending split uses UK-wide PESA Table 5.2 (2021-22 to 2025-26).
  • HMRC income tax Table 2.5 starts in 2023-24; historic Table 2.5a editions are not yet read.
  • DWP Table 1a is Great Britain; the “UK welfare” tab is UK. They are different totals.
  • HMRC receipts and the OBR receipts we use (EFO table 3.8) are both cash. OBR “current receipts” is broader than HMRC’s total because it also includes receipts HMRC does not collect, such as business rates and council tax.
  • DWP Stat-Xplore caseloads, PAYE RTI and household incidence data are not included. The ONS public sector finances and the DWP Table 1c caseloads are included.

Shares: out of every £100

A share says how much of every £100 of a total goes to one thing. Most shares on this site (spending, the home page, debt interest) are built with the rest of the data: the build divides one published figure by the published total for the same year and edition and publishes the result with its own record. The few that are worked out in your browser, such as on the revenue page, use the same division. Either way a share is not a published figure itself, and clicking it shows the formula and links to both inputs.

Per person

“Per person” divides a year’s spending by the ONS mid-year population estimate (30 June) that falls inside that April to March year. We use estimates only, never population projections, so years without an estimate have no per-person figure.

Per household

“Per household” divides a year’s spending by the number of UK households from the ONS Families and households release, which comes from the Labour Force Survey April to June quarter of the calendar year that starts the fiscal year. It counts private households only and is a sample estimate, not a census count. Only UK-wide series get a per-household figure.

Calendar-year and stock series

Some figures are for a calendar year (for example ONS GDP) or are a stock at a date (for example debt at 31 March). An April to March year and a calendar year do not line up, and a stock is not spending over the year, so these series get no real-terms, per-person, per-household or share versions. They are shown as published.

Checking a number yourself

Spreadsheets cannot be linked to a single cell, so the check page gives the file link, its SHA-256 hash (to confirm you have the same file), the sheet, the cell and the steps we applied.
